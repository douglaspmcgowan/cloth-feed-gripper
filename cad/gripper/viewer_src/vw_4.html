The failures are driven by assumed print scatter, not by measured parts, so a printed test coupon decides them. A stack whose verdict depends on another stack inherits that stack's failure.

Two decisions are open: close time and the squeeze cap.

An ESP32 DevKitC runs a table-driven state machine with no blocking waits. Inputs, the force loop, motion, serial and timeouts post events into a 16-slot queue; the last 4 slots are kept for faults and timeouts, and the main loop hands at most 8 events per pass to the state machine.

The force sensor is sampled at 1 kHz and the force loop runs at 50 Hz, one servo frame. The loop is feedforward plus a proportional and integral term, tuned on the stiffest finger in the allowed window, with a cap on the squeeze that keeps any finger in the window at or under 10 N by position alone.

Control law

Readings of the design basis

  • Jaw floor: the design basis says −11°, where the finger analysis shows 12.4 to 14.7 N. The firmware floor is −7.4°, and the squeeze cap is the real limit.
  • Servo rail: 6.5 ± 0.15 V, from the torque analysis and the owner's ruling, not the 6.0 V in the design basis.
  • Pre-close speed: 120 °/s, the basis of the dyno's shock numbers.
  • Grip control: the design basis describes stepping toward the hold angle; the firmware uses the continuous loop above, which integrates below 5.0 N instead of re-stepping below 4.5 N.
  • The dyno analysis still models the earlier loop, so its close and hold numbers are stale against this firmware.

Accept the mass budget miss, or cut weight

Recommended: accept the miss at the 7.4 V kit by explicit ruling and keep the 12 V kit as the fallback. Mass budget

Does the servo count as the DC motor, and the 1:1 pair as the transmission

A hobby servo has a brushed DC motor inside it. If the instructor says no, the fallback is a gearmotor with an 8:1 printed stage, which closes in 0.58 s and needs the close limit relaxed to 0.6 s.

Recommended: ask now; the answer decides whether the fallback is built. Shortlist

Close time: contact or 5 N within 0.5 s

The firmware simulation touches at 0.50 to 0.51 s and reaches 4.5 N at 0.60 to 0.70 s. The torque model alone says 0.278 s.

Recommended: relax the limit to 0.75 s to reach 5 N, keep the 120 °/s pre-close, and prove it with a timed video. Step response

Squeeze cap has zero margin against a finger that folds early

The tip folds at 8.88 N across layers, or 8.41 N with the nail fitted. The firmware's position cap allows 10.0 N on the stiffest finger if the force sensor reads half.

Recommended: keep 10 N as the written limit, lower the cap to a 9 N basis after the bench results, and treat 8.4 N as the working ceiling. Sensor faults

Revision B.1 recesses the housing face around the Ø20 aluminium horn. B18 calls for at least 0.6 mm axially and 1.0 mm radially.
