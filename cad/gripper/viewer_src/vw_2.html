What the model is

The gripper is rebuilt from parameters by one script and exported closed. The arm wrist link and its servo come from the SO-101 upstream model, placed with the horn face mm above the pivots.

Moving the jaw turns side A and side B by equal and opposite angles about their own pivots, 28.5 mm apart, exactly as the CAD script poses them.

How the live mass is added up

It sums the parts that are ticked, each at its own centre of mass along the roll axis, then adds two fixed items: the cloth at the pad (13.5 g at mm) and the sensor and servo wiring (5 g at 40 mm). With every part ticked it matches the fit check: g and g mm.

    Every check is a solid-on-solid measurement in CAD. The moving set includes the dog set screws, the fasteners and the force-sensor stack. The servo horn turns with knuckle A on the servo shaft.

    Jaw travel is sampled in 1° steps plus the firmware floor, wrist roll in 10° steps, and stored distances are rounded to 0.001 mm. For each governed moving pair, the between-sample allowance is half its largest change from the stored minimum to an adjacent pose, plus 0.0005 mm for rounding.

    The roll gap is measured two ways. The ring method treats the fixed wrist link as a full ring at its smallest radius in each band, which is safe but pessimistic; the 3D method turns the wrist link about the roll axis in 10° steps and measures the true closest approach.

    The wrist-flex sweep swings the gripper ±95° at four roll angles against the forearm and compares it with the stock gripper at the same poses.

            The order comes from the CAD: each step's parts travel along their insertion direction, and the path is checked against the parts already fitted. Each screw's driver axis is traced outward from its head to the first fitted part, so a short reach shows up as a finding.

            Torques are assumed starting points for printed parts, not measured values.
