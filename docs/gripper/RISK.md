# Cloth-feed gripper: design failure mode and effects analysis (DFMEA)

Draft 2026-10-11, concept stage, nothing built. Scope: the ME102B cloth-feed gripper on the SO-101 wrist, as drawn in `cad/gripper/`, `assembly/ASSEMBLY.md` and `firmware/gripper/`. `DESIGN-BASIS.md` (DB) wins where this note disagrees. This note ranks what is most likely to hurt the project and names the bench test that would catch each risk.

Tags: **[S1]**–**[S3]** sourced (URL and retrieval date in Sources), **[D file]** derived or quoted from that repository file (**[D SUMMARY.md]** means `analysis/gripper/tolstack/results/SUMMARY.md`, the CAD rev A tolerance results), **[A reason]** assumed. A tag on a table row covers every number in that row up to the next tag. Two requirements are open with Douglas and are **not settled here**: R19 close time (0.5 s as written, 0.75 s proposed) and R15 force cap (10 N as written, about 8 N proposed). Every row that depends on one is written twice, once per value.

## 1. Top ten risks

Ranked by RPN, ties broken by higher severity, then lower id. Occurrence O for F20, F57, F58, F59 and F61 comes from the CAD rev A baseline stack yields in `analysis/gripper/tolstack/results/SUMMARY.md` (see section 2); O for F63 is a judgement [A] checked against the hot-seat stack run. **F20's rank is conditional on the measured horn hole depth** (its O of 10 assumes the CAD 2.5 mm). **The ten rows are not ten distinct risks.** F57 and F58 are one risk (the force peak when the FSR trip fires) scored at the two R15 values, and F59 is the 8 N twin of F35 (force at the jaw floor with the trip failed; F35 is RPN 105, outside the list). ### Trip-peak test (detects F57 and F58)

Not in DB §10 yet; this is the procedure until it is added there.
1. Put an independent force sensor between the pads: a button load cell no thicker than the cloth gap, read by its own amplifier and logger at 1 kHz or faster, or a mechanical peak-hold force gauge. Do not use the gripper's own FSR to judge the peak: the FSR is the instrument in doubt (F28) [A: the trip window is 29.5 ms as shipped and 19.5 ms with `F_TRIP_MS` 10 (`trip_window_s` in `analysis/gripper/tolstack/stacks/basis.yaml` and the T09P trip variants [D]), so 1 kHz gives at least 19 samples inside it; a kitchen scale or the M11 static set-up cannot catch a peak that short].
2. Use the softest and the stiffest finger from the M10 set, one at a time.
3. Run the firmware's normal close (the `G` grip command at the shipped speeds), with the trip set as the firmware will ship. Then repeat with a test build that holds the squeeze on, so the trip has to fire.
4. Close 10 times per finger and record each peak.
5. Fail: any peak above the cap, 8 N as ruled (10 N counted for the historical rows). Report both counts.

**Terms.** FSR: the force-sensing resistor under each pad. TCP: the tool centre point the arm is programmed to. Cpk, yield and worst case are defined in `docs/gripper/analysis/tolerance_stacks.md` (*Words used here*).

## 2. How to read this

Method: a design FMEA, the usual approach for ranking what could fail and how it would be caught. Public description: ASQ, https://asq.org/quality-resources/fmea [S2] (the page returned HTTP 403 to my fetch on 2026-10-11, so it was not read); Wikipedia's FMEA article [S1] was read instead. The standards behind the method are AIAG-VDA (2019) and IEC 60812; their text is not reproduced here. [S1] notes that AIAG-VDA replaced the RPN with an "action priority" table. This note keeps the plain RPN because it is easy to audit by hand, and adds an S ≥ 9 rule so a rare but severe risk is not hidden by a low product.

Each failure mode gets three scores from 1 to 10. **RPN = S × O × D**, so it runs 1 to 1000.

**Severity S: how bad is the effect.**

| S | Meaning (own wording) |
|---|---|
| 1–2 | Nobody notices |
| 3–4 | Small nuisance; the cycle still completes, or a retry fixes it |
| 5–6 | A cycle or seam is lost, or a cheap part is damaged and replaced at the bench |
| 7–8 | Arm or gripper damaged and rebuilt, a needle or machine strike, or force far above the cap |
| 9–10 | Injury risk, or loss of safe control with no warning |

**Occurrence O: how likely is the cause.**

| O | Meaning | Tolerance-stack rows: share of builds out of spec (100 % minus the "Yield (MC)" column) [D analysis/gripper/tolstack/results/SUMMARY.md] |
|---|---|---|
| 1–2 | Not credible in the life of the part | Not used: stack inputs are assumed, so a passing stack is never scored below 3 [A] |
| 3 | Rare; roughly 1 in 10,000 cycles or builds | Passes with Cpk ≥ 1.33 |
| 4 | Roughly 1 in 1,000 | Under 0.01 % out |
| 5 | Roughly 1 in 100 | 0.1 % to 1 % out |
| 6 | Roughly 1 in 50 | 1 % to 5 % out |
| 7 | Roughly 1 in 10 | 5 % to 20 % out |
| 8 | Roughly 1 in 4 | 20 % to 50 % out |
| 9 | Most builds or cycles | Over 50 % out |
| 10 | Certain as drawn | About 100 % out |

The scale has no bucket for 0.01 % to 0.1 % out; such a stack, with Cpk under 1.33, is scored 5 [A: rounded up]. Non-stack occurrence scores are judgement [A] unless the row cites a simulation or test.

**Detection D: how likely the current control is to catch it before it hurts.** High D means it slips through.

| D | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 1–2 | Caught automatically or at once (alarm, boot check, build step that cannot be skipped) |
| 3–4 | Caught by a routine bench test already in DB §10 or the firmware gates |
| 5–6 | Caught only by a special or long test that is not yet planned |
| 7–8 | Caught only by in-use symptoms |
| 9–10 | Not detectable with current means |

**Action rule.** RPN ≥ 100, or S ≥ 9, gets an action. Rows under 100 are watched and rescored after the bench data arrives.

**Where O comes from.** Every stack-driven O now comes from the CAD rev A baseline stack's "Yield (MC)" in `analysis/gripper/tolstack/results/SUMMARY.md` (m 1.5 z 19 gear, a 28.5 mm, pinned layout, recess Ø19.51, holes Ø3.6; N = 100,000 samples, seed 20260922) [D SUMMARY.md]. The earlier provisional marker is gone. Fix variants (the "option" rows of SUMMARY.md) are quoted in the Action column and never set O. `params.json` `wrist_interface` still lists a Ø19.45 pilot and a Ø24.0 hub (lines 488–489, checked 2026-10-11 [D cad/gripper/params.json]); the rev A stacks use Ø19.51 and a hub band at r 11.6 mm, so F21 and F23 name the file as out of step. The stacks are inputs-assumed (FDM scatter is assumed until the coupon is printed), so a passing stack is never scored below 3 [A].

## 4. Findings the tactician should check

1. **An 8 N cap cannot reach 5 N on a soft finger with a position-only cap (F37).** U_MAX falls from 4.867° to 3.785° (the firmware compiled at 8 N, keeping the 0.5 N margin), and fingers softer than about 1.05 N/mm stop short of 5 N. ## 6. Open questions for Douglas

1. **R15 force cap: ruled 8 N (Douglas 2026-10-12); the 10 N / 8 N comparison below is pre-ruling.** At 10 N the finger folds below the cap (8.4–8.9 N), the position cap has only a 0.5 N margin (F11, F36), and the trip peak exceeds 10 N in 14.02 % of builds in stack T09P (F57). At about 8 N the margin is thin, the position cap cannot reach 5 N on soft fingers (F12, F37: 39.50 % of builds reach 5 N; R14 in 9.0 % of simulated grips against 46.7 % at 10 N), and the trip peak exceeds 8 N in 88.47 % of builds with the 7.75 N trip alone (F58); the 4.9° floor (2026-10-16) now holds both F58 and F59 within 8 N in the stacks. The relative floor is now in the firmware (T09F PASS). The stack's trip fix for 10 N (F_TRIP_N 7.0 with F_TRIP_MS 10) was declined in the firmware simulation: it falsely trips normal holds and does not lower the early-contact peaks (F57). At 8 N no trip setting works either: the only trip that passes the stack (5.5 N) sits inside the normal hold band. What lowers the early-contact peaks under both values is a slower approach (controls.md 4.4) or ruling folds out of scope (controls.md O9). Not settled here. Recommendation for whoever decides (the owner keeps both values open): keep 10 N, thicken the finger (F11), and decide O9 (must a fold be gripped); 8 N leaves T09H failing and costs most of R14 [D SUMMARY.md; controls.md 6.4].
2. **R19 close time: 0.5 s, or 0.75 s, and to which event?** The sim reaches contact at 0.503–0.511 s, 4.5 N at 0.589–0.643 s, HOLD at 0.718–0.853 s across the R12 window. At 0.75 s it passes to 4.5 N but misses to HOLD for two of four finger stiffnesses (F33, F34). The stack T09T to first contact is 24.04 % within 0.5 s and 100 % within 0.75 s. Cutting `JAW_SLOW_ZONE_DEG` from 6 to 3 would make 0.5 s pass in the stack, but the firmware simulation declined it: the jaw meets the cloth at up to 41 °/s and an early contact passes 10 N (F33). Recommended: 0.75 s ending at the 4.5 N event.
3. **R20 timing reading (same class as R19).** "Back-off begins within 200 ms" is met with 60 ms spare; "unloaded within 200 ms" is not (F38). Recommended: the first reading.
