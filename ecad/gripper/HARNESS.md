## What to build

1. One sleeved cable, 1200 mm [A, measure at assembly], from the controller board (J3 servo terminal, J4 sensor header) to the wrist: a 20 AWG red/black pair for servo power and five 26 AWG conductors (orange PWM, red +3V3, white FSR, green limit, gray sensor ground).
2. At the forearm end the pair and the PWM wire are spliced to the cut-off stub of a servo extension lead whose male plug mates the servo's own lead; a 470 uF 16 V capacitor is soldered across the splice.
3. The four sensor wires end in a latching 4-position connector (SM) so the gripper detaches; beyond it, two wires go to the FSR through a Clincher connector (never solder to the FSR tail) and two to the jaw-open limit switch (contacts COM and NO).

Pass/fail numbers through X1: the 20 AWG pair costs 79.9 mohm loop resistance and X1 stays inside the 6.35 to 6.65 V hold band at the nominal set point (6.459 V at Fit A hold); a 26 AWG pair would not (6.133 V) [D, harness.txt (b)]. The selected-servo lead is excluded because its gauge and length are not recorded.

### Limit switch contact: COM and NO

Firmware reads the jaw-open limit as a pin pulled to GND (`config.h` `PIN_LIMIT_OPEN`: "switch to GND, INPUT_PULLUP"; `events.h` `EV_LIMIT_HIT`: "switch closed"). R17 (4.7 k) pulls LIM_SIG up on the board. So the switch must close at jaw open: COM to sensor ground, NO to LIM_SIG. A cut or unplugged wire then reads "not at the limit" and can never fake a limit hit. NC would do the opposite. Contact load is 0.70 mA at 3.3 V [D]; check it against the D2F-01L datasheet minimum load before relying on the switch (not read this session) [A].

## Assembly order

1. Measure the route from the board to the wrist with the arm at full reach and at its tightest bend. If it is not 1200 mm, change `cable_len_m` in `ecad/gripper/design.py`, rerun `python ecad/gripper/calc/harness.py`, and cut to the new table.
2. Cut every `spool` wire in the table to length. Mark both ends of each wire with its id (H01 and so on) before anything else; the colours repeat.
3. Board end of the sensor trunk: crimp SXH-001T-P0.6 contacts on H12 to H15 and seat them in the XHP-4 housing at positions 1 to 4. Pin 1 is the +3V3 red wire. Pull each contact to check it is latched.
4. Board end of the power trunk: strip H01, H02, H03 and clamp them in J3 pins 1, 2, 3. Fold the 26 AWG strands back so the screw has more copper. Tug test each.
5. Wrist end of the trunk: crimp SM contacts on H12 to H15 and seat them in the SM plug. Crimp SM contacts on the wrist-side wires H16 to H19 and seat them in the SM receptacle at the same positions. Mate once and confirm the latch clicks.
6. Servo splice: cut the female end off the extension lead 972, leaving a 100 mm pigtail with the male plug. Slide heat-shrink over each wire. Solder H01 to the black pigtail wire (H04), H02 to the red wire (H05), H03 to the white wire (H06). Solder the capacitor leads across the red and black splices, + to red and - (stripe) to black, leads trimmed to about 10 mm [A]. Shrink the sleeves.
7. Braided sleeve: thread it over the five trunk wires and the two power wires before the second end is terminated, that is after step 3 and before steps 5 and 6 (the expanded sleeve may not pass over a finished SM housing or the splice [A]), so it covers H01 to H03 and H12 to H15 end to end. Secure its ends with a tie and a short heat-shrink band so it cannot ride back.
8. FSR end: terminate H16 and H17 on the Clincher connector according to Amphenol's instructions. Never solder to the FSR tail: soldering heat destroys the tail. Press the tail into the Clincher.
9. Switch end: solder H18 to the NO terminal and H19 to the COM terminal of the D2F-01L (terminals are marked on the case) [A].
10. Run the continuity checks below before any power.

## Strain relief

What the CAD has: the servo sits on `front_plate` and `wrist_mount` (PETG, four M3x6 into the horn); finger B turns on its knuckle and carries the FSR in `fsr_backing_b` (tail slot 7.6 mm wide) (`cad/gripper/params.json`, `drawings/BOM.md`). The CAD has no cable anchor, no sleeve clamp, no Clincher seat and no limit-switch pocket. Until rev B adds them, use the temporary method below and request the features under "Requested CAD changes".

Temporary anchoring until rev B:

1. Anchor the sleeve end on the fixed part: tie it with a 2.5 mm zip tie [A] to the `wrist_mount` ribs or the servo body at the side away from the jaws, so the sleeve cannot move. All movement then happens in the loose wire beyond the sleeve.
2. Service loop: beyond the sleeve end, leave the wire free in one open loop of diameter at least 30 mm, i.e. bend radius at least 15 mm (10 x 1.5 mm OD [S flex_wire]; 7.5 mm is the absolute minimum). Never lay a wire in a tighter bend than 15 mm, including at a tie.
3. The SM connector sits at the loop's fixed side so the gripper can detach without touching the sleeve.
4. Finger side: tie H16 and H17 to the finger-B web near the root with a tie, then to the Clincher, so the wires move with the finger and bend only in the free span across the knuckle.
5. The span across the knuckle sees 27.4 deg of bending (jaw -7.4 to +20 deg [config.h, DESIGN-BASIS R18]). At 15 mm radius that is 7.2 mm of slack taken up and given back each cycle [D]; leave at least that much, with the loop not touching the pivot.

Check the wrist-flex swing too (SO-101 wrist flex, DESIGN-BASIS R9): the loop must not pinch between the flex parts at +/-95 deg [A that the CAD envelope check applies].

## FSR lead flex-life

The FSR 402 tail carries printed silver traces that crack if flexed, and soldering heat destroys the tail [S: sourcing.EVIDENCE['fsr_tail'], Interlink guidance not readable in the session, [A]]. The data sheet gives up to 10 million actuations of the sensing area, not of the tail. The lead past the Clincher moves on every jaw cycle: 100000 of them over the design life [A].

Mitigation:

1. Clincher, not solder: the tail goes into the Clincher and is never heated.
2. Bond or tie the tail and the Clincher to the finger-B backing so neither moves relative to the finger. Only the silicone wire (H16, H17) bends, and only in the free span across the knuckle.
3. Stranded silicone wire (26 AWG, 1.4 to 1.5 mm OD) and the 15 mm minimum radius above.
4. Keep the service loop at or above 15 mm radius at all jaw angles; check it by eye at open (+20 deg) and closed (-7.4 deg).
5. Inspect every 10000 cycles (about 97 h of use, one semester [A, DESIGN-BASIS section 7]): look for a crack or kink at the Clincher crimp and at the knuckle span; check continuity of H16, H17 flexed by hand. Replace the wire at the first break; do not repair in place.
